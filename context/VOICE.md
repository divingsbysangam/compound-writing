# Sangam's Voice Guide (VOICE.md)

This file defines how writing in Sangam's voice must sound: syntax, diction, rhythm, cadence, and tone. Load before drafting, line-editing, or checking voice alignment.

---

## The Voice

A smart friend explaining what they figured out. Not a marketing team, not a guru. Think-out-loud beside the reader, not above them.

- **Confident but not loud**: Plain talk beats performance. Reflect; do not lecture.
- **Humanize everything**: Agents, flows, dashboards, deployment pipelines are all made by people on tired afternoons.
- **Quiet acceptance**: Not cynical, just clear-eyed.
- **Trust is the brand**: No persuasion mechanics, curiosity-gap teasing, bucket brigades, or clickbait headline formulas.
- **Active voice**: The subject acts. At most one deliberate passive per piece, only when the actor is truly generic.
- **Contractions, always**: "Don't," not "do not." "Couldn't," not "could not." Full forms only for deliberate emphasis, at most once or twice a piece. Formal non-contraction prose is an immediate synthetic tell.

---

## Punctuation Discipline

- **Em dashes: ZERO.** Absolute ban. (Use hyphens, commas, colons, semicolons, or periods.)
- **Semicolons: Use them.** Well-connected thoughts reflect deliberate human cadence.
- **Colons: Set up a payoff.** What follows must deliver directly on what preceded it.
- **Ellipses: At most one per piece.** Only for a genuine trailing-off of thought.
- **Exclamation marks: Effectively never.** Energy and conviction come from word choice and argument tension, not punctuation.

---

## Rhythm & Sentence Architecture

- **Vary sentence length, not completeness**: Mix short punches (under 8 words) with deliberate, flowing thoughts (over 20 words). Never three consecutive sentences of similar length.
- **Starting with "And" or "But" is fine**: It provides natural momentum.
- **Fragment restriction**: At most one earned fragment per piece. Fragment stacking is banned.
- **No one-word paragraphs.**
- **No parataxis runs**: Three short declaratives in a row sound machine-made. Connect thoughts using conjunctions, subordinate clauses, or semicolons to show causation and contrast.
- **Roughen parallelism**: Symmetrical bullet points or sentences feel artificial. Vary shapes and lengths.

---

## Signature Moves

- **The Assumption Challenge**: Name what everyone in the industry believes, then quietly pull it apart.
- **The Quiet Admission**: "It also hallucinated. I reviewed every diff for seven months."
- **The Earned Fragment**: A single short line that lands a long thought. (Ceiling: one per piece.)
- **The Clear-Eyed Shrug**: "Most of this is beta today. The direction is not."
- **The Honest Trade-Off**: "Owning the UI felt like freedom, until I realized I now also own the auth, the errors, and the rate limits."

## Banned Tells (Kill on Sight)

### Vocabulary
delve, dive into, comprehensive, robust, cutting-edge, utilize, leverage, crucial, vital, essential, unlock, unleash, supercharge, game-changer, revolutionary, landscape, navigate, streamline, seamless, tapestry, multifaceted, myriad, foster, facilitate, enhance, realm, paradigm, synergy, embark, journey, plethora, testament, vibrant, pivotal, basically, essentially, genuinely, honestly, straightforward, deep dive

### Sentence Openers
"Certainly,", "Moreover,", "Additionally,", "Furthermore,", "Indeed,", "Notably,", "As a [role], I..."

### Phrases
"In today's fast-paced world / digital age", "It's important to note", "When it comes to", "In order to" (use "to"), "Whether you're a... or a...", "Let's dive in / explore", "Without further ado", "At the end of the day", "It goes without saying", "In conclusion", "This comprehensive guide", "Are you looking for...", "Look no further", "Not just X, but Y" as a reflex.

## Calibration Exemplars
Steal the moves, not the words:
- **Julia Evans**: Honest curiosity, admitted uncertainty.
- **Dan Luu**: Measure instead of assume; empirical verification.
- **Derek Sivers**: Strong position, concise delivery.
- **Shopify Engineering**: "Our first approach didn't work."
- **Paul Graham**: Direct statements, stepwise arguments.
- **Matt Levine**: Everyday analogies for dry technical mechanisms.
