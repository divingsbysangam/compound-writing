# Sangam's Style Guide (STYLE.md)

This file defines what writing for *Divings by Sangam* must accomplish, contain, and prove: substantive standards, article structure, platform packaging, and publication readiness.

---

## Two Modes. One Voice.

### Reflective Mode
- Focus: Productivity, mindset, stillness, system-of-work pieces.
- Posture: Think-out-loud beside the reader. Reflect, do not lecture.
- Landing: Lands on feeling or perspective shift.

### Technical / Field-Tested Mode
- Focus: Salesforce, AI tools, agentic workflows, architecture build logs.
- Posture: Drop into the moment; let the scene carry the weight. "I deployed. Here is what happened. No pretense. Just what broke first."
- Landing: Lands on action or mechanism.

---

## Pre-Drafting Invariants

1. **Write to one person, not a segment**: Picture a specific reader you could name: an experienced engineer at a particular point in their week, facing a specific dilemma. Schwarz awareness: they are solution-aware; skip the throat-clearing introduction.
2. **One arguable claim per piece**: Not one topic, one claim. It must be something a reasonable peer could dispute. If two claims compete, the second one is the next issue.
3. **The So What? Chain**: Push "so what?" until the point reaches something the reader lives with; write from the bottom.
4. **Drafts default to a scaffold, not finished prose**: Substack checks authorship detection. An agent generates beats, statement headings, tensions, and the facts each section must carry. Sangam writes the sentences. A full prose draft serves only as a structural proof.

---

## Substack Short-Form Mode (600–900 words)

Four-part arc:
1. **Hook**: Core tension or paradox within 2–3 sentences (First Paragraph Rule).
2. **Story**: A real moment or concrete build breakdown.
3. **Shift**: The counterintuitive truth.
4. **Takeaway**: One clean, defensible idea.

Headings: Statement headings using `##`.

---

## Substack Long-Form Story Mode (1,300–1,400 words)

A story that happens to teach, built on six disciplined beats:

1. **Cold open inside a memory, not a claim**: Specific time, place, physical action.
2. **The problem is felt before it is named**: Two or three scenes of symptom before diagnosis.
3. **The turn arrives through an artifact**: A document, a release note, an error log, an announcement. The story earns the teaching.
4. **The framework is taught inside the story (first person)**: No second-person lecturing or generic textbook inserts.
5. **The framework diagnoses the narrator first**: Apply the lens to Sangam's own work, including where it stings, before generalizing.
6. **Close on meaning plus action**: Single action or clear feeling.

**Live Scene Rule**: Exactly two or three dramatized close-ups (dialogue, micro-actions, objects, time markers) sourced ONLY from Sangam's real memory via interview. Never decorate or invent scene details.

---

## Headings
Statement headings that carry weight on their own.
- **Good**: "None Of It Was Me" · "The Front Door Moved" · "The Demo That Died Quietly"
- **Banned**: Generic labels ("Introduction", "Overview"), questions as headings ("Why does it matter?"), headings that only describe.

---

## LinkedIn Caption Mode (Koe/Shipper Principles)

- **Source**: Derived directly from the Substack essay.
- **Opener**: One-line quotable bold claim (8–14 words) OR a concrete first-person moment with an uncopyable detail.
- **Body**: 1–2 full sentences per paragraph. Subject-first; filler words cut (*actually*, *sitting*, *by then*). Leave room for a wry, self-including aside.
- **Close**: Soft bridge to the carousel. **No closing question** (Sangam cuts them).
- **Hashtags**: Exactly 3–4 PascalCase hashtags on the final line (e.g., `#SalesforceDev #AgenticAI`).
- **Links**: Substack link goes in the FIRST COMMENT, never in the post body.

---

## Pre-Delivery Checklist
```
[ ] One arguable claim, stated in a single sentence, and only one
[ ] The claim is falsifiable: a reader could argue it is wrong
[ ] Written to one picturable reader, not a segment
[ ] Every paragraph makes the next one necessary
[ ] No banned words, openers, or phrases
[ ] Contractions used naturally throughout
[ ] No parataxis runs; no three same-length sentences in a row
[ ] Not everything in threes; no perfect parallelism; paragraph shapes vary
[ ] At least one direct opinion, one real number, one admitted limit
[ ] No em dashes; at most one ellipsis; effectively no exclamations
[ ] Active voice; at most one deliberate passive
[ ] No fragment stacks; at most one earned fragment
[ ] Nothing invented: every scene, number, and quote traceable to Sangam or a source
[ ] Read aloud: sounds like Sangam talking to a smart friend
[ ] Could anyone else have written this? If yes, add the uncopyable detail.
```
